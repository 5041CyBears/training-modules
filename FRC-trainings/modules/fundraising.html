Fundraising & Sponsor Engagement

Module Goals

By the end, students should be able to:

  • Explain why fundraising is part of team sustainability, not just “asking for money.”
  • Describe how students can engage in sponsor conversations with confidence and professionalism.
  • Use a simple structure for making an ask, telling the 5041 story, and following up.
  • Apply general sales skills to campaigns such as Honey Den candy sales.
  • Understand why outreach, recruitment, and fundraising can happen together at community events.
  • Choose a meaningful fundraising role that fits their strengths and helps the team.
Why It Matters

Fundraising keeps the program open, prepared, and growing

Access

Fundraising helps reduce barriers so students can participate in robotics without every cost falling on families.

Capability

Money becomes robot parts, tools, registration, travel, outreach supplies, and learning opportunities.

Sustainability

Strong support lets the team plan beyond one season and invest in future students.

Key idea: Students are not just raising money. They are building trust with the community.

Sponsor Conversations

What potential sponsors need to hear

Tell the team story

  • Who we are and who we serve.
  • What students build and learn.
  • How support helps students directly.
  • What the sponsor can do next.

Use a clear ask

“Would your business consider supporting us with a sponsorship, materials, tools, food, printing, or help connecting us with others?”

Sales Campaigns

Honey Den candy sales are practice for real communication

Know the product

Be able to explain what is being sold, the cost, and how the money supports the team.

Make it easy

Keep the ask short, polite, and clear. Respect a “no” and thank people anyway.

Track accurately

Handle money, inventory, orders, and delivery carefully so the campaign stays trustworthy.

General skill: Candy sales, event concessions, sponsor outreach, and donation drives all use the same habits: clarity, confidence, honesty, and follow-through.

Sales Skills

A good sales interaction is short, respectful, and specific

Example flow

Greeting: “Hi, I’m with 5041 CyBear Robotics.”

Purpose: “We’re selling candy from the Honey Den to support robot parts, travel, tools, and STEM outreach.”

Ask: “Would you like to buy one or donate to the team?”

Close: “Thank you for supporting local robotics!”

Check for Learning

Choose the strongest sales response

A person says, “I do not really need candy.” What is the best response?

Choose one answer.

Combination Events

Some events fundraise, recruit, and do outreach at the same time

Fundraising

Collect donations, sell items, run concessions, host a dinner, or run a community activity.

Outreach

Show the robot, explain FIRST, teach STEM activities, and represent the team.

Recruitment

Invite students, families, mentors, sponsors, and volunteers to join or support 5041.

Examples include robot demonstrations at community events, Hoover Days activities, STEM camps, fairs, school nights, and sponsor open houses.

Event Planning

Design events with clear student jobs

Before the event

  • Define the goal: money, outreach, recruitment, or all three.
  • Assign student roles and shifts.
  • Prepare talking points, flyers, and supplies.
  • Practice the robot demo or sales pitch.

After the event

  • Count money and return inventory.
  • Record contacts and follow-up tasks.
  • Post a thank you or send notes.
  • Debrief what worked and what to improve.
Why Participation Matters

Fundraising is a team responsibility

It is not just for business team

Mechanical, programming, awards, media, and drive team students all benefit from funded tools, parts, travel, and events.

It builds professional skills

Students practice communication, confidence, planning, follow-through, public speaking, money handling, and community leadership.
