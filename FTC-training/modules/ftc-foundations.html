What Is FIRST Tech Challenge?

FTC basics, FIRST values, and the Iowa season pathway

By the end, students should be able to:

  • Explain what FIRST Tech Challenge is and why teams do more than build robots.
  • Describe the basic parts of an FTC event: judging, inspection, matches, pits, and awards.
  • Apply FIRST Core Values, Gracious Professionalism, and Coopertition to team decisions.
  • Describe the Iowa FTC season pathway from kickoff to league meets, league tournament, and championship.
  • Identify how 5041-supported FTC teams should prepare, compete, document, and reflect.
FTC Basics

FTC is a student-centered robotics program

Students Build

Teams design, build, wire, program, test, and drive a reusable robot for a new yearly challenge.

Students Compete

Robots play head-to-head matches with alliance partners while teams also present their work to judges.

Students Lead

Students take roles in engineering, programming, business, media, outreach, judging, safety, and strategy.

Key idea: FTC is not just a smaller robot. It is a complete team experience where students practice technical, communication, leadership, and career-ready skills.

Team Roles

Every student can find meaningful work

Mechanical

Prototype, CAD, fabricate, assemble, repair, and improve robot mechanisms.

Programming

Configure robot hardware, write autonomous and driver-control code, use sensors, and debug.

Business

Budget, fundraise, communicate with sponsors, organize files, and help the team plan sustainably.

Outreach

Document impact, create media, support recruitment, present to judges, and connect with the community.

5041-supported FTC teams should live the same standard as the CyBears: Everyone has a place here.

Competition Basics

FTC events combine robot performance and team presentation

Inspection & Pit Work

Teams check in, organize a pit, complete robot and field inspections, and repair between matches.

Matches

Teams play qualification matches with alliance partners. Match success depends on reliability, strategy, driving, and teamwork.

Judging & Awards

Teams explain engineering, outreach, business, and team growth through interviews, portfolios, pit conversations, and awards.

Match Flow

Most matches have two major control phases

Autonomous

The robot runs pre-written code. Teams use sensors, odometry, vision, or timed actions to score without driver control.

Driver-Controlled

Student drivers operate the robot, communicate with their alliance, score game pieces, defend safely, and adapt to the match.

Exact timing, scoring, penalties, and endgame tasks change every season. The Competition Manual and official updates are always the source of truth.

FIRST Values

The six FIRST Core Values shape how teams compete

Discovery

We explore new skills and ideas.

Innovation

We use creativity and persistence to solve problems.

Impact

We apply what we learn to improve our world.

Inclusion

We respect each other and embrace our differences.

Teamwork

We are stronger when we work together.

Fun

We enjoy and celebrate what we do.

Gracious Professionalism

High-quality work and respect go together

What it looks like

  • Listen to mentors, volunteers, referees, and other teams.
  • Do careful work even when time is short.
  • Give credit, share knowledge, and own mistakes honestly.

What it does not look like

  • Blaming others publicly.
  • Mocking another team or teammate.
  • Using pressure, shortcuts, or unsafe behavior to win.
Coopertition

Teams can compete hard and still help each other

Before Matches

Help alliance partners understand strategy, field position, and robot capabilities.

In the Pit

Share spare parts, tools, or advice when another team is stuck and time allows.

After Events

Celebrate improvement, thank volunteers, and keep learning from other teams.

Season Overview

The FTC season moves from learning to competing

Preseason

Recruit, register, train, learn the tools, organize roles, inventory parts, and practice basic skills.

Kickoff

Watch the game reveal, read the manual, study scoring, and begin strategy and robot requirements.

Build & League Meets

Prototype, program, test, document, compete in league meets, and improve after each event.

Tournaments

League tournaments and championships include judging, inspection, matches, awards, and advancement.

Iowa FTC

Iowa uses leagues, meets, and league tournaments

League Assignment

Teams are assigned to Iowa leagues. Sister teams from the same organization are usually placed together when possible.

League Meets

Teams attend multiple shorter meets. These matches help teams practice, improve, and build standings before the tournament.

League Tournament

All league teams attend a tournament with judging, inspection, qualification matches, alliance selection, playoffs, and awards.
